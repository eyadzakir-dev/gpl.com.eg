05

How import clearance works.

Sea import · Egypt · 6 steps

In 2024 the average container at Alexandria took 8.6 days to leave the port. Published free time is often only 5–6 days. The gap is closed before the ship arrives: registration, documents and HS codes are settled while your cargo is still at sea.

Source: Egyptian Customs Authority, Time Release Study 2024; carrier tariffs, 2026.

  1. 01

    Pre-register on Nafeza (ACI)

    At least 48 hours before your cargo leaves the export country, the shipment is registered on Nafeza, Egypt’s trade single window, and gets a 19-digit ACID number. Your supplier uploads the invoice, bill of lading, packing list and certificate of origin on CargoX, and the ACID goes on every document. No ACID, no clearance.

  2. 02

    Check documents and HS codes

    Names, HS codes, values and weights must match across the ACID, invoice, bill of lading and certificate of origin. We check them, confirm any permits, and agree the HS codes with you, so duties and import rules are known before arrival.

  3. 03

    Arrival: clearance starts

    The vessel discharges and the free-time clock starts. Since June 2026, clearance can begin on arrival without waiting for the shipping line’s delivery order, so we start the same day and collect the delivery order in parallel.

  4. 04

    Customs declaration

    We file the customs declaration electronically and pay duties and VAT on your behalf, or you pay directly. Either way you see every amount before it is paid.

  5. 05

    Inspection and release

    Customs examines the cargo where required, along with any agency checks (for example food safety or import control). Once cleared, the container is released.

  6. 06

    Gate-out and delivery

    We truck the container to your door and return the empty to the line’s depot before free time runs out.

Steps and timing vary by cargo type, port and agency. ACI also applies to air imports since 1 January 2026. We tell you up front which steps apply to your shipment.
